Tiramisu

Description

Tiramisu is a traditional Italian dessert without cooking which literally means “pick me up,” an apt description for the dessert, given that the combination of robust espresso and cocoa provides energy. The recipe for the dish comes from the Veneto region of Italy. Crisp ladyfinger biscuits (savoiardi) are quickly dipped in sweetened espresso—frequently spiked with Marsala wine or dark rum—and layered between a velvety, lightened cream made from whipped eggs, sugar, and rich mascarpone cheese.

The key to making a good tiramisu is moderation – the bitterness of the strong roast coffee and bitter unsweetened cocoa perfectly balances the creamy texture of the mascarpone. The key to its preparation is placing it in the fridge for at least six hours, which allows the dry ladyfingers to soak in the coffee thoroughly enough and become fluffy. Dusted with a final layer of cocoa powder just prior to consumption, the dessert serves up a frosty mix of coffee and cream that has made it popular the world over for years.

Ingredients

  • 500g Mascarpone
  • 100-110g Sugar
  • 4 Eggs
  • 300ml Espresso Coffee
  • 300g Ladyfingers
  • Few Spoons of Cocoa Powder

Steps

  1. Prepare the coffee that is very strong. Do not add sugar. Let the coffee cool down before dipping the ladyfingers.
  2. Seperate eggs' whites and yolks into two seperate bowls. Whisk the yolks with the sugar for 4-5 minutes. Add mascarpone and whip gently.
  3. Whip the egg whites with a pinch of salt until it looks like whipped cream. Add it into the mascarpone mix and stir gently.
  4. Layer the tiramisu. Spread a few spoons of the mascarpone cream on the bottom of the baking dish. Dip each half of the ladyfingers for 1 second, and place them onto the mascarpone layer. Spread half of mascarpone cream onto the ladyfingers' layer. Repeat the process until you finish all of the ingredients. The top layer must be the mascarpone cream layer.
  5. Dust the tiramisu's top layer with cocoa powder and let it rest for five hours.
  6. After resting, the tiramisu is now ready to be served.
